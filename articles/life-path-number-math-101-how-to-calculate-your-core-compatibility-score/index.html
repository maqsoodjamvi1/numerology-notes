When I first started looking into numerology to figure out why a past relationship felt like a constant uphill battle while a friendship felt completely effortless, I realized I needed to move past vague horoscope descriptions and look at the actual math. If you are trying to decide whether to invest time in a new romantic partnership, a business venture, or even a roommate situation, calculating a core compatibility score using Life Path numbers gives you a surprisingly clear framework to work with.

You do not need an advanced math degree or expensive software to figure this out. You just need a pen, a piece of paper, and the full birth dates of the people involved. Here is how I break down the math step-by-step so you can run the numbers for yourself today.

Step 1: Reduce Your Birthday to a Single Digit

Before you can compare two people, you have to find each person’s individual Life Path number. The method is straightforward, but there is one specific rule about master numbers that trips a lot of people up.

Let us use a real-world example. Say someone was born on November 14, 1988 (11/14/1988).

  • First, reduce the month: November is the 11th month. In standard numerology reduction, 11 is a master number, but for basic Life Path calculations, we often reduce double digits unless you are working with an advanced chart. Let’s keep it simple: 1 + 1 = 2. (Note: If you prefer keeping master numbers 11, 22, and 33 intact, you can, but for standard compatibility math, reducing to a single digit is the baseline). Let’s stick to reducing everything down to 1 through 9 for clean comparison.
  • Next, reduce the day: 14 becomes 1 + 4 = 5.
  • Finally, reduce the year: 1988 becomes 1 + 9 + 8 + 8 = 26. Then, add those digits together: 2 + 6 = 8.

Now, add your three reduced numbers together: 2 (month) + 5 (day) + 8 (year) = 15. Reduce that final sum: 1 + 5 = 6.

This person’s Life Path number is a 6. Repeat this exact same process for the second person you want to evaluate. Let’s say the second person calculates down to a Life Path 3.

Step 2: Calculate the Core Compatibility Score

Once you have both Life Path numbers—in our example, a 6 and a 3—you look at how they interact. Instead of relying on rigid “good or bad” labels, numerology looks at the relationship as its own energetic entity.

To find the core compatibility score, you use a simple arithmetic combination of the two Life Path numbers:

  1. Add Person A’s Life Path number to Person B’s Life Path number.
  2. Reduce that sum to a single digit between 1 and 9.

Using our example: 6 (Person A) + 3 (Person B) = 9.

In this system, the resulting number—in this case, 9—represents the overarching theme, challenge, and potential of the partnership. A 9 partnership is naturally humanitarian, creative, and focused on the bigger picture. If you calculated two numbers and got a 2, the partnership theme would center on patience, partnership, and subtle diplomacy.

Step 3: Evaluate the Dynamic Using Your Results

Once you have your core score, you can look at the raw friction points between the individual numbers. Certain numbers naturally complement each other, while others require conscious effort to avoid friction.

  • The Builders (4 and 8): These numbers focus on structure, stability, and hard work. When paired together, they create an unstoppable operational force, but they risk turning the relationship into a strict business partnership if they forget to play.
  • The Visionaries (1, 3, and 5): These numbers crave independence, change, and self-expression. Two visionaries together will never run out of conversation, but they might struggle to pay the bills on time or stick to a long-term plan without grounding energy.
  • The Nurturers (2, 6, and 9): These numbers prioritize harmony, community, and emotional support. They create deeply comforting environments, but they run the risk of codependency or avoiding difficult direct conversations to keep the peace.

Take a look at the two original Life Path numbers you are comparing. Do you share a modality (like two visionaries), or are you balancing opposing forces (like a 4 and a 3)? Recognizing this dynamic helps you understand whether a disagreement stems from a personal flaw or simply a fundamental difference in how your core numbers process the world.

Running these calculations takes less than five minutes, but it completely changes how you view relational dynamics. Instead of guessing why a dynamic feels heavy or frictionless, you have a mathematical baseline to explain it.
